Dance Pattern Mapper

Keep what you learn in class.

DPM is a pattern library for partner dancers and the people who teach them. Collect the figures from your course with your own videos, see how they connect, and share the list with your classmates or your students.

View on GitHub Take the tour

In active development for Android and iOS. Open source, no account needed.

Made for the dance course

A course teaches a new figure every week, and by the last week the first ones have faded. DPM gives what you learn a place to stay.

For dancers

Film each figure while it's fresh, add the teacher's tips, and keep a reference you can come back to long after the course ends.

Share the list with your classmates and take turns recording the latest figure, so nobody has to film every week.

For dance teachers

Keep one list per group and add each figure as you teach it, with a tag for each course. Your students subscribe with a code, and their copy keeps growing as the group moves on from one course to the next.

Your course material, digital and always at hand, without building a website or handing out logins.

See how your figures connect

Some figures are foundations of the dance. In West Coast Swing, the sugar push, the left side pass, the underarm turn and the whip each stand on their own. Others grow out of them: the sugar tuck out of the sugar push, the tuck turn out of the left side pass, the basket whip out of the basic whip. You record which figure builds on which, and DPM draws the map.

  1. Start a list for your dance

    Pick a template for West Coast Swing, Salsa, Bachata, Argentine Tango or Lindy Hop, or start from a blank list for any other partner dance.

  2. Add each figure as it comes

    Name, type, counts and rhythm, level, notes, your videos from class, tags such as the course or festival, and the figures it builds on.

  3. Look it up, map it, watch it

    The list for looking things up, the map for how it all connects, and reels for watching your videos one after another.

One list, three ways to see it

Open a list and the bar at the bottom switches between three views of the same figures. The menu holds everything else: your other lists, and Settings.

  • List Every figure with its type and level. Sort it, filter it, open one for the details.
  • Map Which figure builds on which, as a timeline or a network.
  • Reels The videos of your figures, one per screen.

Open a figure and you get its counts and rhythm (1 2 3&4 5&6 for a six-count pattern), its notes, its videos and its variations.

Two views of one map

On the Map tab, the timeline gives each pattern type its own lane and flows left to right by how many steps a pattern is from the basics. The network is a zoomable graph you can pan, pinch and arrange by hand.

  • Colours show the pattern type, shading shows the level
  • Badges show which patterns have videos or variations
  • Drag nodes into your own arrangement. DPM remembers it for each list
  • Tap any pattern to open its details and videos

Find a pattern, and everything around it

Filter by name, type, level, counts or tags. On the graph, the filter doesn't just hide what doesn't match. It can keep the path that leads to a match, or everything connected to it, in view. The matches stay bright and the rest fades back.

Back from a festival weekend with more figures than you can remember? Tag them, filter for the tag in the weeks after, and you see each new figure together with the ones it builds on.

The same filter works on the list, alongside sorting by name, type, level, counts or date added.

Variations without duplicates

"With an inside turn", "with a free spin", "hesitation": modifiers change how a pattern is danced without making it a new pattern. Each one comes before, after or inside the pattern it's attached to.

  • Universal modifiers apply to every pattern in the list and can carry demo videos of their own
  • Others attach to individual patterns and can carry their own demo videos per pattern: that pattern danced that way
  • A modifier's page lists every pattern it's used with

DPM doesn't come with videos. Every clip is one you or your class recorded, or a link you added.

Share a list with your class

Teachers publish a course list and get an 8-character code and a QR code to show in class. Students who scan it get a live copy that grows every time a figure is added. It's read-only for them, and becomes theirs to keep and edit if you stop sharing. A published list carries video links, not files, so link clips you've put on a video site.

Classmates can pass a list round instead. Export it to a file with your videos inside and send it on, and whoever records the next figure adds it and sends the list on again. You can mark an export read-only, and imports are checked before anything is written, so a damaged file can't half-overwrite your data.

Reels: your figures, one video at a time

The Reels tab gathers every pattern that has a video. The overview shows a still of each; tap one to watch them one per screen. Swipe up or down to the next pattern, and sideways through its videos: its own first, then those of it danced with a modifier.

  • Reels from the list you have open, or from every list on your phone
  • The phone's own video controls, with fullscreen in landscape
  • Back to the overview with the grid button, or with Android's back

Before class or a social, flick through what you've learned so far. Reels only plays the videos already in your lists: the ones you recorded, and the links you or your teacher added.

On your phone, not in the cloud

Your class videos, put to work

Attach videos to any pattern: a clip from your gallery, a new recording, or a YouTube link that starts at the right second. Then let DPM help with the tedious part:

  • Shorten a long recording to just the pattern
  • Anonymize a clip by hiding the dancers' faces and looks, so everyone in it can be comfortable with it being shared
  • Transcribe what the teacher says, then tap a line to jump to it, or add the lines you want to your notes
  • Suggest a name and description from that transcript. You decide whether to use it

All of it runs on the phone. The speech and language models download once, and your videos never leave the device. Anonymizing, transcribing and suggestions are available on Android first.

And the details

One list per group or dance

Each list has its own pattern types, colours and modifiers. The five dance templates come with starter patterns.

Levels, counts, rhythm and tags

Beginner to advanced, the counts and rhythm of a pattern, and free-form tags to filter by. Pick the dance and DPM suggests the rhythms that fit the counts.

Works offline

Everything is stored on your phone. Cloud sharing is optional and only happens when you publish.

Nine languages

English, 中文, हिन्दी, Español, Français, العربية, বাংলা, Português and Deutsch. DPM follows your phone's language by default.

Two styles, light and dark

After Hours is warm and roomy; Clipboard is dense enough to keep a whole course on one screen. Each in light and dark, with contrast that meets accessibility minimums. This page has both: switch at the top.

Questions

Which dances does it work for?

Any partner dance. There are templates for West Coast Swing, Salsa, Bachata, Argentine Tango and Lindy Hop, and a blank list lets you set up your own pattern types for anything else.

I teach a course. How do I set it up?

Create a list for the group, add each figure as you teach it, and tag it with the course. Publish the list and show the code or QR code in class. Your students' copies follow every change you make, so the next figure appears on their phones as soon as you add it, and the list carries on when the group moves up to the next course.

How can my class share the filming?

Agree who records which week. Either one person keeps and publishes the list and links the clips once they're on a video site, or you pass the list round as an exported file with the videos inside, each of you adding the figure you filmed. Anonymize a clip first if not everyone wants to be recognisable in it.

Do I need an account?

No. You never create an account or sign in. Your lists live on your phone. Sharing works with a code, not a profile; when you publish a list, the app quietly gets an anonymous ID so that only you can change or remove it.

Where do my videos go?

Nowhere, unless you send them. Videos stay on your device. An export can include them if you choose, and a published list only shares video links, never the files.

Does the AI send my videos to a server?

No. Anonymizing, transcribing and suggesting all run on the phone. The models download once (Wi-Fi recommended), and you can delete them in Settings.

What happens to a list I've shared if I stop sharing?

The cloud copy is deleted right away. People who subscribed keep their copy, and it becomes theirs to edit.
