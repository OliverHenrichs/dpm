DPM alpha 1.0.0

Thanks for joining the closed test. DPM helps you keep track of the figures you learn or teach: what each one is, how it is counted, what you need to know first, and videos of it. This is the first build anyone outside development is using, so expect rough edges, and please report them.

What you can do

Lists of figures

  • Make a list per dance or group, and let it grow from course to course. Start blank or from a template (West Coast Swing, Salsa, Bachata, Argentine Tango, Lindy Hop) with a few starter figures and types.
  • Each figure has a name, type, counts, an optional rhythm and level (beginner, intermediate, advanced), a description, tags, prerequisites and videos.
  • Rhythm is written the way dancers count it, for example 1 2 3&4 5&6 for a six-count West Coast Swing pattern, and the app suggests the dance's basic rhythms for the counts you entered.
  • Tags work well for a whole course or a festival weekend, so one list can grow over the years.

Variations as modifiers

  • A variation is a modifier on a figure rather than a new figure: a whip with an inside turn is the basic whip plus “with inside turn”.
  • Some modifiers apply to every figure (a hesitation, a hand change) and carry their own demo videos. Others are attached to single figures, each with videos of that combination.

Map

  • See how figures build on each other, as a timeline (one lane per type) or as a network you can pan, zoom and arrange by hand.
  • Search or filter and the Map shows the matches together with the chain that leads to them.
  • Prerequisite loops are pointed out instead of silently breaking the picture.

Reels

  • Flip through every figure that has a video, one per screen: up and down between figures, sideways through a figure's videos. Handy for a quick refresh before a social.
  • Browse the open list or all your lists at once.

Videos

  • Add a video from your gallery, record one with the camera, or paste a link (YouTube included, with an optional start time). You can also start a new figure from a video.

Video tools (Android, all on your phone)

Open a figure, go to Videos and tap Edit video:

  • Shorten cuts a clip down to the part you need.
  • Anonymize turns up to 30 seconds into a silhouette video: tap the dancers and they are tracked through the clip, so you can share a figure without showing faces. The original stays in your gallery.
  • Transcribe speech writes down what the teacher says. Tap a line to jump there, or add lines to the description.
  • Suggest name and description drafts both from the transcript. Nothing changes until you tap Use suggestion.

Transcription needs a one-time download of about 61 MB, suggestions about 1.3 GB and a phone with at least 6 GB of RAM. Manage both under Settings, On-device models. No video, audio or transcript leaves your phone. Long jobs run in the background and send a notification when done.

Sharing

  • Publish a list to get a short code and a QR code. Others subscribe with either and their copy stays up to date. Subscribed copies are read-only, and only you can update or stop sharing your list. No account is needed.
  • Or export lists to a file (with or without videos, optionally read-only) and import them on another phone. Transcripts are left out unless you choose to include them.

Make it yours

  • Two styles: After Hours (the default) and Clipboard (denser, for running a course), each in light and dark.
  • App icon in indigo, amber or coral.
  • Nine languages: English, Chinese, Hindi, Spanish, French, Arabic, Bengali, Portuguese and German. The app follows your phone's language until you pick one.

Known limitations

  • Android only for now; an iPhone version comes later.
  • Arabic text reads correctly, but the layout is not yet mirrored right to left.
  • Suggestions need a phone with 6 GB of RAM or more and are hidden on smaller phones.
  • Your lists live on your phone. Export them now and then as a backup, since uninstalling the app can delete them.

What helps most

  • Anything that crashes, freezes or loses data, with what you did just before.
  • Places where you didn't know what to tap next, or a word didn't make sense.
  • Dance terms or starter figures that are wrong for your dance.
  • How the video tools work on your phone model: speed, result, battery.
